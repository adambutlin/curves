# Structural origins and the propagation of sovereign yield-curve movements

*Research design. Written 4 October 2026. This programme supersedes the descriptive
study of the 2026 gilt selloff, which was stopped on the same date; the 2026 episode
is now an out-of-sample application of the general model, not the object of study.*

---

## 1. The question

A 15bp rise in the 10-year yield is not an economic shock. The same move can be
generated by monetary-policy repricing, stronger growth, inflation news, a change in
risk compensation, domestic fiscal or sovereign risk, global risk, or a foreign
spillover. Write the day-$t$ reduced-form yield innovation as a sum of structural
contributions,

$$u^y_t = u^{MP}_t + u^{Growth}_t + u^{Inflation}_t + u^{Risk}_t + u^{Foreign}_t + \cdots$$

Event commentary usually stops once this decomposition is estimated. The question
here is what comes after it:

> **Does knowing why yields moved today contain information about how the yield
> curve subsequently evolves?**

Formally, for the same observed initial move $\Delta y_t = c$, does

$$E_t[\Delta y_{t+h} \mid \Delta y_t = c,\ \varepsilon^{MP}_t] \neq E_t[\Delta y_{t+h} \mid \Delta y_t = c,\ \varepsilon^{Growth}_t] \neq E_t[\Delta y_{t+h} \mid \Delta y_t = c,\ \varepsilon^{Risk}_t]\ ?$$

If so, the structural composition of today's move is a state variable for tomorrow's
curve.

**Hypotheses.**

- $H_A$: the persistence and propagation of sovereign yield movements depend on their
  structural origin.
- $H_0$: conditional on today's information and the initial yield move, structural
  origin contains no incremental information about subsequent bond dynamics.

**Architecture.**

$$\text{cross-asset prices } X_t \xrightarrow{\text{structural BVAR}} p(S_t \mid \mathcal I_t) \xrightarrow{\text{conditional propagation}} p(R_{t+h} \mid S_t, \mathcal I_t)$$

where $S_t$ is the vector of identified structural shocks on day $t$, represented
probabilistically because sign restrictions only set-identify it, and $R_{t+h}$ is the
outcome over the following $h$ trading days (yield continuation or reversal, level,
slope, curvature, cross-country spreads, excess returns).

**Methodological lineage.** The decomposition layer follows the daily sign-restricted
VAR literature: Cieslak and Pang (2021, *JFE*) for US stocks and the Treasury curve,
and Brandt, Saint Guilhem, Schröder and Van Robays (2021, ECB WP 2560) for the
cross-Atlantic system. Giannone and Primiceri supply the discipline of estimating
before an unusual episode and asking which disturbances rationalise the forecast
errors, and of comparing prior with posterior to show that a decomposition is learned
from data rather than imposed by restrictions. The decomposition is not the
contribution; it is the state-extraction layer.

**Minimum viable project.** One market (US Treasuries), three or four shocks, daily
structural shocks, shock-conditional dynamics at 1, 5, 10 and 20 trading days,
explicit conditioning on the size of the initial move, comparison with
momentum/reversal and curve-state benchmarks, pseudo out-of-sample estimation, and
2026 held out completely. Term-premium mechanism, richer taxonomy, UK and German
markets, relative value and nonlinear propagation follow only if the MVP works.

**Failure conditions** (the project is abandoned or redesigned if any holds):
shocks cannot be robustly identified; labels change under small changes in
restrictions; posterior decompositions reproduce the priors; origin has no
incremental relation with subsequent dynamics; results vanish once the initial move
is conditioned on; predictability is ordinary momentum/reversal; results exist in
one crisis only; pseudo out-of-sample results fail; 2026 results need ex-post changes
to parameters or restrictions.

## 2. Assessment of the design (findings, 4 October 2026)

### 2.1 In a linear VAR the forecast does not depend on the identification

Let $u_t$ be the vector of reduced-form VAR innovations (one per variable) and $B$
the impact matrix selected by the sign restrictions, so that the structural shocks are
$\varepsilon_t = B^{-1}u_t$ with $E[\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_t'] = I$ and $BB' = \Sigma$.
Consider the predictive regression of the proposal,

$$r_{t,t+h} = \alpha_h + \beta_h \Delta y_t + \gamma_h' S_t + \delta_h' Z_t + e_{t+h},$$

with $S_t = \varepsilon_t$ the full shock vector and $Z_t$ conventional predictors.
Because $B$ is invertible,

$$\operatorname{span}(\Delta y_t, S_t, Z_t) = \operatorname{span}(\Delta y_t, u_t, Z_t)$$

for **every** admissible $B$, including a wrong one. Fitted values and out-of-sample
losses are identical across rotations; only the labels on $\gamma_h$ change. A
rejection of $\gamma_h = 0$ therefore says that today's equity (and FX, breakeven)
innovations predict future yields given today's yield change: a reduced-form fact. A
random rotation predicts exactly as well, so the placebo test in the original
validation list would fail by construction, and identification uncertainty would
never reach the forecasts.

Within the VAR itself, $E[\Delta y_{t+h} \mid \varepsilon^k_t]$ is simply the
cumulative impulse response at horizon $h$. Local projections and VARs estimate the
same object in population (Plagborg-Møller and Wolf, 2021), so "shock-conditional
propagation" is a statement about impulse-response shapes rather than a separate
forecasting object.

### 2.2 What the null actually tests, and where identification enters

The null of the proposal is identification-free. "Origin is irrelevant given the
initial move" means that the subsequent change depends on today's innovations only
through the observed yield change:

$$H_0:\ r_{t,t+h} = \alpha_h + \beta_h \Delta y_t + \delta_h' Z_t + e_{t+h} \quad\text{against}\quad r_{t,t+h} = \alpha_h + \beta_h \Delta y_t + \delta_h' Z_t + \phi_h'\tilde u_t + e_{t+h},$$

where $\tilde u_t$ collects the other reduced-form innovations. Equivalently, in shock
space, $\gamma_{h,k} = \pi_h\, b_{y,k}$ for every shock $k$, with $b_{y,k}$ the impact of
shock $k$ on the yield: every source of a 1bp move is followed by the same fraction
$\pi_h$ of further movement. This is good news for the paper: **the test of $H_0$ is
robust to identification uncertainty.** Identification matters for describing the
alternative (which origins continue and which reverse) and for any hypothesis that
uses fewer labelled objects than there are innovations. Three tests follow.

1. **Reduced-form content (identification-free).** Does $\tilde u_t$ predict
   $r_{t,t+h}$ given $\Delta y_t$ and $Z_t$? This is necessary for any linear version
   of $H_A$. If it fails, $H_A$ fails under every identification.
2. **Shock-specific propagation ratios.** For each shock $k$,
   $\pi_{k,h} = \gamma_{k,h}/b_{y,k}$ is the fraction of the day-$t$ move caused by $k$
   that continues ($\pi > 0$) or reverses ($\pi < 0$) over the next $h$ days. Report
   the distribution across the identified set; the economic claim is robust only if
   the ranking of $\pi_{k,h}$ survives across admissible rotations.
3. **A parsimonious structural state.** Split the day-$t$ innovation into the part
   due to shocks to short-rate expectations and the part due to risk-premium shocks,
   $u^y_t = C^{EH}_t + C^{TP}_t$, and test whether the two parts propagate differently.
   Because the split is a two-dimensional projection of a four-dimensional innovation,
   its fit depends on the rotation, so this is a genuine test of the identification.
   It is benchmarked against random rotations with random groupings. It is also the
   formal version of the expectations-versus-term-premium question.

Nonlinear functions of shocks (size, sign asymmetry, regimes) are also
rotation-dependent and are the natural extension, not part of the MVP.

### 2.3 Position relative to the literature

Cieslak and Pang (2021) already identify growth, monetary, common-premium and
hedging-premium shocks from daily changes in the 2-, 5- and 10-year Treasury yields
and the stock market, and they already run local projections of multi-horizon yield
changes on those shocks (their Section 5.1: premium shocks are long-lived, monetary
shocks mean-revert within about a year). Their estimates are full-sample (look-ahead),
condition on nothing but the shocks, and their error bands exclude set-identification
uncertainty. Brooks, Katz and Lustig document weeks-long post-FOMC drift in Treasury
yields, prior evidence that at least one origin propagates. The contribution here must
therefore be: (i) conditioning on the observed initial move; (ii) real-time
estimation; (iii) forecast evaluation against momentum, curve-state and reduced-form
benchmarks; (iv) set-identification uncertainty carried into every result; (v) the
expectations versus term-premium channel; (vi) the international extension with
domestic versus foreign origin.

### 2.4 Statistical power

With serially uncorrelated shocks, the standard error of a drift coefficient is
approximately $\sigma_h/\sqrt T$, where $\sigma_h$ is the volatility of the $h$-day
yield change and $T$ the number of trading days. With daily 10-year volatility of
about 6-7bp, $\sigma_{20} \approx 27$-$31$bp. On 1983-2025 ($T \approx 10{,}700$) the
standard error is about 0.3bp, so a 20-day drift of roughly 0.6-1bp per one-standard-
deviation shock is detectable. On 2026 alone ($T \approx 190$ days by early October)
it is about 2bp. **2026 can validate the decomposition; it cannot test the propagation
hypothesis.** The paper says so.

### 2.5 The 2026 holdout is already partly seen

The descriptive study's design work (20 August 2026) looked at 2026 data:
between 27 February and 19 August 2026 the UK 10-year rose 77bp (frozen ACM: +61bp
expected rates, +14bp term premium), the US 10-year rose 68bp (NY Fed published ACM:
+51bp expected rates, +17bp term premium) and the Bund 10-year rose 57bp; sterling was
flat, the gilt-OIS spread unchanged and the 30-year breakeven up only 8bp. Brent peaked
at $138 on 7 April and was back near $95 in August. Any restriction chosen after that
is chosen with this knowledge. The procedural remedy: the identification, data,
transformations, priors, horizons and tests are committed in
[02-identification-preregistration.md](02-identification-preregistration.md) before any
estimation, and the estimation data layer refuses to return observations dated 2026
or later unless the holdout is explicitly unsealed.

### 2.6 What "learned from the data" can mean under sign restrictions

Under a uniform (Haar) prior on the rotation, the likelihood is flat in the rotation:
the data update only the reduced-form covariance $\Sigma$, never the rotation itself
(Baumeister and Hamilton, 2015). A Giannone-Primiceri prior-versus-posterior comparison
must therefore ask whether the decomposition statistics move when the estimated
cross-asset covariance replaces a covariance with no cross-asset information. If the
variance shares are much the same either way, the decomposition is produced by the
restrictions, not learned.
